## The Short Answer
The hemp ban is not a ban on CBD. Starting December 11, 2026, a hemp product with more than 0.4 mg of total THC in the whole container no longer counts as hemp under federal law. A CBD product with 0.4 mg of total THC or less in the container is still hemp. The cap catches most full spectrum CBD sold today.

## The Law and the Dates
* **Law:** Public Law 119-37, the federal funding law passed in November 2025. It rewrites the definition of hemp.
* **November 12, 2026:** Cannabinoids the hemp plant cannot make on its own (synthetic cannabinoids) no longer count as hemp. This date was not delayed.
* **December 11, 2026:** Hemp moves to a total THC standard, which counts THCA and other THC-like cannabinoids, not only delta-9 THC. Finished products are capped at 0.4 mg of total THC per container. A funding bill in September 2026 moved this date back from November 12.
* **Further Delay:** Several bills in Congress would delay or rewrite the rule. None had passed as of November 25, 2026.
* **Products Already at Home:** The law does not address them. It is written around what counts as hemp and what can be sold as hemp.

## Why Full Spectrum CBD Is Affected
Mark Gillilan pulled the lab reports for 8 batches of full spectrum gummies and oils from three well known brands. The CBD to THC ratio ran from about 24:1 to 40:1, with one batch at 91:1.

| CBD to THC ratio | Most CBD a container could hold and stay under the cap | THC in a typical 1,500 mg full spectrum bottle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 24:1 | About 10 mg | About 63 mg, roughly 156 times the cap |
| 30:1 | About 12 mg | About 50 mg, roughly 125 times the cap |
| 40:1 | About 16 mg | About 38 mg, roughly 94 times the cap |
| 91:1 | About 36 mg | About 16 mg, roughly 41 times the cap |

A full spectrum product can only stay under the cap if it has very little CBD in it. Broad spectrum and isolate products are made to remove THC and can land under the cap. Only the lab report shows it for a given product.

## How to Check a Product
1. Find the lab report (COA) that matches the lot number on the product.
2. Add delta-9 THC plus 0.877 times THCA.
3. Convert that to milligrams for the whole container.
4. Compare it to 0.4 mg.
5. If the result is non-detect (ND), use the lab's detection limit as the worst case. If the worst case is under 0.4 mg, the product clears the cap.

The live page has a CBD cabinet checker that does this math from a lab report, or gives an estimate from the label alone. It is an estimate, not a legal determination.

## How to Keep Buying CBD After December 11, 2026
* Ask for total THC per container in milligrams, not a percent.
* Look for non-detect (ND), then check the detection limit.
* Match the lab report to the lot number.
* Treat full spectrum with a normal amount of CBD as a red flag after December 11, 2026.
* Check state rules. Some states are stricter than the federal law.

## What Else the Rule Affects
* **Delta-8, THCA and THC drinks:** These are the main products the law was written to stop. Almost none still count as hemp under the new standard.
* **States with legal marijuana:** Licensed dispensaries operate under state marijuana laws, which this rule does not rewrite. Products over the cap can no longer be sold as hemp outside that system.
* **Drug testing:** Mark's answer is not to take CBD, including Kyoto Botanicals products, if a drug test is possible. Non-detect is not a promise about how a drug test will read.
